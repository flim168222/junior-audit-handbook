# Cash Audit

## 1. Definition

Cash represents the company's most liquid assets, including cash on hand and cash held in bank accounts.

---

## 2. Financial Statement Classification

**Statement of Financial Position**

Category: Current Asset

---

## 3. Audit Objective

To obtain reasonable assurance that cash balances are:

- Existing
- Complete
- Accurate
- Properly presented in the financial statements

---

## 4. Relevant Assertions

| Assertion | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Existence | Cash balances actually exist |
| Completeness | All cash transactions are recorded |
| Accuracy | Amounts are correctly recorded |
| Rights and Obligations | Company owns the cash |
| Cut-off | Transactions are recorded in the correct period |

---

## 5. Common Audit Risks

- Cash theft or unauthorized withdrawals
- Incorrect bank reconciliation
- Unrecorded bank transactions
- Overstatement of cash balance

---

## 6. Audit Procedures

### Bank Confirmation
Confirm cash balances directly with financial institutions.

### Bank Reconciliation Testing
Compare bank statements, cash book, and reconciliation items.

### Subsequent Transaction Review
Review transactions after year-end to identify unusual items.

### Cash Count
Perform physical cash count when applicable.

---

## 7. Audit Evidence

- Bank confirmation
- Bank statements
- Bank reconciliation
- Cash book
- Cash count sheet

---

## 8. Working Paper Examples

- Cash Lead Schedule
- Bank Reconciliation Testing
- Confirmation Control Sheet

---

## 9. Interview Questions

**Q: Why do auditors perform bank confirmation?**

A:
Bank confirmation provides reliable external evidence to verify the existence and accuracy of cash balances.
