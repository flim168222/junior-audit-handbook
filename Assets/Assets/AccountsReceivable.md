# Accounts Receivable (AR) Audit

## 1. Definition

Accounts Receivable (AR) represents amounts owed by customers for goods sold or services provided on credit.

AR is classified as a current asset because it is generally expected to be collected within the company's normal operating cycle.

---

## 2. Financial Statement Classification

**Statement of Financial Position**

Category: Current Asset

---

## 3. Audit Objective

To obtain reasonable assurance that accounts receivable balances are:

- Existing
- Complete
- Accurate
- Collectible
- Properly presented in the financial statements

---

## 4. Relevant Assertions

| Assertion | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Existence | Customer receivable balances actually exist |
| Completeness | All receivables are recorded |
| Accuracy | Receivable amounts are correctly calculated |
| Valuation | Receivables are stated at recoverable amounts |
| Cut-off | Revenue and receivables are recorded in the correct period |
| Rights and Obligations | The company has the legal right to collect amounts owed |

---

## 5. Common Audit Risks

- Fictitious sales and fake customers
- Revenue recognized before goods are delivered
- Customers unable to pay outstanding balances
- Incorrect allowance for doubtful accounts
- Incorrect year-end cut-off

---

## 6. Internal Controls

Examples of controls over accounts receivable:

- Credit approval process before selling on credit
- Customer credit limit monitoring
- Regular review of aging reports
- Approval of sales transactions
- Reconciliation between subsidiary ledger and general ledger

---

## 7. Audit Procedures

### Accounts Receivable Confirmation

Send confirmation requests directly to customers to verify outstanding balances.

### Subsequent Cash Receipt Testing

Review payments received after year-end to evaluate the collectibility of receivables.

### Aging Analysis Review

Analyze outstanding balances based on payment periods and identify overdue accounts.

### Sales Transaction Testing

Select sales transactions and inspect supporting documents:

- Sales invoice
- Delivery note
- Customer contract

### Cut-off Testing

Test sales transactions before and after year-end to ensure revenue and receivables are recorded in the correct accounting period.

---

## 11. Interview Questions

**Q: Why do auditors perform accounts receivable confirmation?**

A:

Auditors perform AR confirmation to obtain external evidence supporting the existence and accuracy of customer balances.

---

**Q: What is the difference between vouching and tracing in revenue testing?**

A:

Vouching starts from accounting records and checks back to supporting documents to test occurrence.

Tracing starts from source documents and follows them into accounting records to test completeness.
